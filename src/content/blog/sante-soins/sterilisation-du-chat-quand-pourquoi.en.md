# Cat Spaying: When, Why, and Recovery

Spaying is an essential step in your four-legged companion's life, offering benefits for its health and the well-being of the entire feline community. Discover when to perform this procedure, why it's crucial, and how to support your cat during its convalescence.

## When to spay your cat?

The ideal age for spaying is between 4 and 6 months, before the first heat cycle in females and before adulthood for males. This period helps prevent undesirable behaviors (marking, roaming) and the risk of diseases. Consult your veterinarian for a personalized schedule, as certain breeds or cats in poor health may require different timing. Early spaying significantly reduces the risk of mammary tumors and uterine infections in females.

## Why is spaying beneficial?

The benefits are numerous, for both your cat and you. In females, it eliminates stressful heat cycles, the risk of pyometra (uterine infection), and mammary tumors. In males, it prevents aggressive behaviors, roaming, and urinary tract diseases. Socially, spaying reduces feline overpopulation and abandonment. A spayed cat is often calmer, more affectionate, and in better overall health.

## How does the procedure and recovery go?

Spaying is a routine surgical procedure performed under general anesthesia. For females, the ovaries and uterus are removed (ovariohysterectomy), while males undergo an orchidectomy (testicle removal). The surgery lasts about 20-30 minutes. Recovery takes 7 to 10 days: keep your cat calm, check the incision daily, and limit its jumping. A protective collar may be necessary to prevent it from licking its suture. Follow your veterinarian's advice for a smooth recovery.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will spaying make my cat gain weight?**  
A: No, as long as you adjust its diet. Spaying can slow down metabolism, so opt for a balanced diet and control portions to prevent weight gain.

**Q: My cat is old, can it still be spayed?**  
A: Yes, although the surgery is simpler in younger cats. A pre-operative health assessment is essential to evaluate the risks in senior cats.
