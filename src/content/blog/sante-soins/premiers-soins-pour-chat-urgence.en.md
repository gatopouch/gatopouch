# First Aid for Cats: What to Do in an Emergency

A cat can get injured at any time, at home or outdoors. Knowing how to react quickly can save its life, but certain actions should be avoided to not worsen the situation.

## Common Emergencies and First Steps

### Bleeding
If your cat is bleeding heavily, immediately apply a clean, dry cloth to the wound for 5 minutes. Do not use cotton, which could leave fibers. The bleeding should stop or decrease significantly. If the blood is bright red and the wound continues to bleed a lot after compression, contact your veterinarian immediately.

### Burns
In case of a burn (boiling water, chemical), immediately rinse the affected area with lukewarm water for 10-15 minutes. Avoid ice cubes or products without veterinary advice. Then cover with a clean cloth and consult a professional.

### Fall from a Height
After a fall, your cat may have internal or external fractures. Do not move it if it seems to be in pain or unconscious. Gently place it in a blanket or basket and transport it to the vet without shaking.

## Poisoning: React Quickly

A cat can be poisoned by toxic plants (ivy, tulip), household products (bleach, lye), or dangerous foods (chocolate, onion). Identify the substance ingested and call your veterinarian or the Poison Control Center immediately. Never give a home remedy without professional advice.

## Preparing Your First Aid Kit

Always have a first-aid kit within reach that includes:
- Sterile compresses
- An elastic bandage
- A saline solution (for eye rinsing)
- Your veterinarian's number and the Poison Control Center's number.
Regularly check the kit to verify the condition of the products.

### FAQ

**Q: Should I give my cat aspirin for pain?**  
A: No! Aspirin is toxic to cats. Only a veterinarian can prescribe a suitable painkiller.

**Q: What should I do if my cat stops breathing?**  
A: Check its mouth, remove any object, perform artificial respiration (mouth to nose) and a gentle cardiac massage before rushing it to the vet.
