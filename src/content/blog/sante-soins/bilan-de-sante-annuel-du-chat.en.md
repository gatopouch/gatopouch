# The Cat's Annual Health Check-up: Recommended Exams

An annual health check-up is essential for early detection of any potential health problems in your cat. As a responsible owner, you can play a key role in its prevention and well-being.

## Complete Veterinary Consultation

The annual visit begins with a thorough clinical examination. The vet will assess your cat's overall condition: its weight, coat, eyes, ears, and mouth. They will also check its mobility, heart rate, and breathing. This is the opportunity to discuss any changes in behavior, appetite, or habits, even minor ones. A cat is a master of disguise, so your vigilance is valuable in helping the vet identify any potential signs of pain or illness.

## Biological and Parasitic Check-up

A blood test is a recommended exam, especially for cats over 7 years old or those showing symptoms. It allows for checking kidney, liver, and thyroid function and for detecting any possible infection or anemia. The search for intestinal parasites through a stool analysis is also crucial, even for an indoor cat. These exams help establish a baseline health status and track your companion's health over time.

## Vaccinations and Oral Health

Even if your cat doesn't go outside, some vaccinations remain important for its protection. The vet will assess its needs based on its lifestyle. At the same time, the dental exam is often overlooked, but oral problems can cause pain and more serious complications. A cleaning under anesthesia may be recommended if necessary. Good dental hygiene, with special kibble or suitable toys, is a good complement to the annual visit.

### FAQ

**How often should a cat have a health check-up?**  
An annual check-up is ideal for all cats, starting from the age of 1. For seniors (over 7 years) or cats with chronic illnesses, two visits a year are often recommended for closer monitoring.

**Preparing for the vet visit: what should you bring?**  
Bring the vaccination record, results from previous exams, and a list of any medications or supplements given. Also, note any behavioral or dietary observations since the last visit to ensure you don't forget anything.
