Cat vaccines are essential to protect your feline companion from potentially serious diseases. By following a suitable vaccination schedule, you provide them with an effective defense barrier while ensuring their longevity and well-being. Here is a complete guide to help you understand it better.

## Why vaccinate your cat?

Vaccination is one of the cornerstones of feline health prevention. It helps prevent viral or bacterial infections that can be fatal, especially for kittens or senior cats. Vaccines stimulate your cat's immune system, enabling it to effectively fight off specific pathogens.

### Commonly prevented diseases by vaccination

- **Rabies**: Mandatory in many countries, this disease is transmissible to humans and fatal.
- **Feline Panleukopenia**: A highly contagious viral infection, often fatal for kittens.
- **Feline Leukemia**: Weakens the immune system and can lead to cancers.
- **Feline Herpesvirus (Rhinotracheitis)**: A common respiratory infection, particularly uncomfortable for cats.

## The recommended vaccination schedule

The cat vaccination schedule varies based on age, lifestyle, and exposure to risks. Here are the key steps to follow:

### Core vaccines (essential)

These vaccines are recommended for all cats, whether they live indoors or outdoors.

1. **First injection**: From 8 weeks of age for kittens.
2. **Booster**: At 12 weeks, then annually or every 3 years depending on the type of vaccine (monovalent or combined).

### Non-core vaccines (optional)

These are reserved for cats exposed to specific risks, such as outdoor cats or those that frequent shelters.

- **Chlamydia**: Prevents eye and respiratory infections.
- **Feline Infectious Peritonitis (FIP)**: Recommended for cats living in groups.

## Booster shots: why are they important?

Booster shots are crucial for maintaining your cat's immunity. Without them, the protection can wane, making your cat vulnerable to diseases. Consult your veterinarian to establish a personalized vaccination program, especially if your cat has health issues or a particular lifestyle.

## Practical tips before and after vaccination

For the vaccination to go smoothly, follow these tips:

- **Before vaccination**: Ensure your cat is healthy. A veterinary examination beforehand is recommended.
- **After vaccination**: Watch for local reactions (swelling, pain) or general reactions (fatigue, fever). Contact your veterinarian if symptoms persist for more than 48 hours.

## FAQ

### Can my cat have side effects after vaccination?
Yes, but they are generally mild and temporary (mild swelling, lethargy). Rare cases of allergic reactions may occur, which is why it's important to monitor your cat after the injection.

### Do older cats need vaccines?
Yes, senior cats should follow a tailored vaccination schedule, as their immune system is more fragile. Discuss this with your veterinarian to adjust the boosters.

### Are vaccines mandatory for all cats?
Rabies is mandatory in many regions. Other vaccines are recommended based on your cat's risk of exposure.
