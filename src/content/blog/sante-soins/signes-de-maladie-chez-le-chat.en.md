# Signs of Illness in Cats: 10 Symptoms to Watch For

Cats are masters of disguise. In the wild, showing weakness means becoming a predator's prey. This is why detecting the signs of illness in your cat is crucial for acting in time. Here are the 10 symptoms that should alert every cat owner.

## 1. Loss of Appetite

A cat that refuses to eat for more than 24 hours should be seen by a vet. Loss of appetite can indicate:

- Dental problems (inflamed gums, broken tooth)
- Digestive upset
- Kidney disease
- Significant stress

**Attention**: A cat that doesn't eat for 48 hours risks developing hepatic lipidosis, a serious liver disease.

## 2. Change in Behavior

Behavior is the first indicator of a cat's health. Watch for:

### Increased Changes

- Excessive restlessness
- Unusual meowing (especially at night)
- Increased attention-seeking

### Decreased Changes

- Lethargy, excessive sleeping
- Isolation (hiding under the bed)
- Loss of interest in play

## 3. Urinary Problems

Urinary problems are common and can be urgent. Signs to watch for:

- Difficulty urinating (straining in the litter box)
- Blood in the urine
- Frequent urination in small amounts
- Painful meowing while urinating

**Life-threatening emergency**: A male cat that cannot urinate must see a vet immediately (urinary obstruction = life-threatening emergency).

## 4. Vomiting and Diarrhea

An isolated vomit can be normal (hairball). But:

- More than 2 vomits in 24h → vet consultation
- Diarrhea for more than 48h → vet consultation
- Blood in the stool → emergency
- Vomiting with a fever → emergency

## 5. Change in Weight

Sudden weight loss (more than 10% of body weight) is a warning sign. Weigh your cat regularly:

| Sign | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Sudden loss | Vet consultation |
| Sudden gain | Reduce portion sizes |
| Gradual loss | Analyze diet |

## 6. Respiratory Problems

Watch for:

- Rapid breathing at rest (> 30 breaths/minute)
- Breathing through the mouth (very abnormal in cats)
- Frequent coughing or sneezing
- Nasal or eye discharge

## 7. Change in Coat

A dull, puffed-up, or patchy coat can indicate:

- Nutritional deficiencies
- Thyroid problems
- Chronic stress
- External parasites (fleas, ticks)

## 8. Lethargy and Weakness

A normally active cat that becomes lethargic for more than 24 hours should be examined. Possible causes:

- Anemia
- Fever
- Chronic pain
- Infectious disease

## 9. Limping or Difficulty Moving

Limping in cats is often related to:

- Injury (fall, fight)
- Arthritis (especially in senior cats)
- Infection (abscess)
- Fracture

## 10. Bad Breath (Halitosis)

Persistent bad breath can signal:

- Dental problems (tartar, gingivitis)
- Kidney disease
- Digestive problems
- Diabetes (acetone smell)

## When to See an Emergency Veterinarian

Some signs require an immediate visit (within 2 hours):

- Difficulty breathing
- A cat that is prostrate and unresponsive
- Inability to urinate (especially in males)
- Visible trauma (fall, accident)
- Seizures
- Bleeding

## How to Prepare for the Vet Visit

1. Note the symptoms with their start dates
2. Take photos if relevant (litter box, eyes, gums)
3. Bring the health record and vaccination history
4. Prepare a dietary history

## FAQ

### My cat has been eating less for 2 days, should I be worried?

If no other symptoms are present, monitor for 48 hours. If the loss of appetite persists or is accompanied by other signs (lethargy, vomiting), see a vet.

### How do I take my cat's temperature?

A normal cat temperature is between 38°C and 39°C. Use a digital rectal thermometer with lubricant. If the temperature exceeds 39.5°C, see a vet.

### My senior cat is sleeping much more, is this normal?

Senior cats (7+ years old) can sleep up to 18 hours a day. But if the lethargy is accompanied by a loss of appetite or a change in behavior, see a vet.

## Conclusion

Knowing the signs of illness in your cat can save its life. Cats hide their pain out of survival instinct, so you must be attentive to subtle changes. When in doubt, never hesitate to consult your veterinarian—it's better to be safe than sorry.
