# Worming your cat: frequency and recommended products

Worming your cat is essential for its health and well-being. Discover the ideal frequency and the best products to protect your companion from intestinal parasites.

## Why worm your cat?

Intestinal worms, such as roundworms, hookworms, or tapeworms, are common in cats, especially those that go outdoors or hunt. These parasites can cause digestive issues, weight loss, and even serious complications in kittens or immunocompromised cats. Deworming also prevents the risk of transmission to humans, especially children. A dewormed cat is a healthier, more energetic, and happier cat.

## Deworming frequency by age

The frequency of deworming depends on age, lifestyle, and the risk of infestation.
- **Kittens**: From 3 weeks of age, then every 15 days until 2 months, then monthly until 6 months.
- **Adult cat**: Every 3 months if the cat goes outdoors, otherwise twice a year.
- **Senior or at-risk cat**: Follow your veterinarian's advice to adjust the frequency.
Regular check-ups with your veterinarian are recommended to adjust the treatment according to your cat's needs.

## Recommended deworming products

Several types of dewormers are available: tablets, spot-on treatments, or syrup. Products containing **praziquantel** or **febantel** are effective against most worms. Spot-on treatments, like those from GatoPouch, are convenient for easy application and less stressful for your cat. Prefer products prescribed by your veterinarian, who will know how to recommend the molecule suited to your cat. Avoid "all-in-one" dewormers without medical advice, especially for kittens or cats under 1 kg.

## Signs of infestation to watch for

Certain symptoms may indicate a parasitic infestation:
- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Swollen abdomen or weight loss
- Presence of worms in the feces
- Lethargy or loss of appetite
If you notice these signs, consult your veterinarian quickly for a diagnosis and appropriate treatment.

### FAQ

**Q: My cat never goes out, does it need to be dewormed?**
A: Yes, even indoor cats can contract worms (eggs brought in on your shoes or via other animals). Annual deworming is recommended.

**Q: Can you deworm a 2-month-old kitten?**
A: Yes, kittens should be dewormed from a very young age, following a specific protocol. Consult your veterinarian for a suitable schedule.
