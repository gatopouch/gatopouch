Music isn't just for humans: it can also transform your cat's well-being. Discover how feline music therapy helps reduce stress and create a harmonious environment at home.

### The Benefits of Music for Cats
Cats have a particularly well-developed sense of hearing and are sensitive to specific frequencies. Music designed for them can lower their heart rate, reduce anxiety, and even improve their sleep. Studies show that melodies composed with slow tempos (between 120 and 140 beats per minute) and high frequencies mimic the soothing sounds kittens hear in their early life, thus promoting a sense of security. For optimal effect, limit sessions to a maximum of 30 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day, especially during stressful events like thunderstorms or travel.

### Recommended Musical Styles
Some music is more effective than others for calming felines. **Adapted classical** compositions (such as works by Bach or Debussy) or **melodies specifically designed for cats**, like those from the "Music for Cats" project, are ideal. These tracks use soft violins and pentatonic scales, which are more natural to the feline ear. Avoid overly loud or rhythmic genres like rock or electronic music, which can increase your four-legged companion's stress.

### How to Integrate Music into Your Cat's Routine
For music therapy to be effective, create a consistent ritual. Play the music during meals, playtime, or before leaving the house. Place a speaker at a distance from your cat's bed or favorite spot so as not to startle it. If your cat reacts positively (purring, relaxation, rumbling), you've found the right frequency! Test different playlists to see what best suits its personality.

### FAQ
**Q: Does ordinary classical music work for cats?**  
A: Yes, but only if it is slow and without sudden changes. Pieces with fast tempos or crescendos can have the opposite effect. Opt for calm and repetitive pieces.

**Q: My cat seems indifferent to the music. What should I do?**  
A: Some cats have personal preferences. Try different frequencies or natural sounds like rustling leaves. Persistence is key: observe its reactions and adjust accordingly.
