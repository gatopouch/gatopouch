# Carrying Your Cat Close: Why Affective Carrying Strengthens Your Bond

The cat has long been considered an independent animal that "doesn't like cuddles." This is a misconception. More and more studies show that cats are deeply social animals that benefit greatly from physical contact with their human. Affective carrying is one of the most enriching practices for a cat's well-being.

## The Cat, a Social Animal That Needs Contact

### The Misconception of Independence

For a long time, the cat (independent) was contrasted with the dog (affectionate). This view is incorrect. The cat is a social animal that:

- Forms lasting bonds with its peers
- Seeks contact with its human
- Shows affection through purring, head-butting
- Suffers from loneliness just like a dog

### The Benefits of Physical Contact

Regular contact between a cat and a human brings:

- **Reduced cortisol** (stress hormone)
- **Increased oxytocin** (bonding hormone)
- **Body temperature regulation**
- **Nervous system calming**

## The Benefits of Carrying for the Cat

### 1. Stress Reduction

A cat carried against its human feels secure. The human's heart rate (60-80 bpm) acts as a biological cradle that calms the feline's nervous system.

### 2. Strengthening the Bond

Regular carrying for 15-30 minutes a day creates a lasting bond. The cat associates your scent, warmth, and rhythm with security.

### 3. Stimulating Purring

A cat's purring has therapeutic virtues. It emits frequencies between 25 and 50 Hz that:

- Promote bone healing
- Reduce inflammation
- Also calm the human's nervous system

### 4. Preventing Boredom

A carried cat discovers the world from an elevated, mobile viewpoint. This stimulates its brain and reduces boredom (a common issue for indoor cats).

## The Benefits for the Human

### Reduced Anxiety

Petting a purring cat against you reduces your cortisol by 30% in 10 minutes. Prolonged carrying acts as an active meditation.

### Lower Blood Pressure

Cat owners have lower blood pressure than average. Regular contact is a key factor.

### Combating Loneliness

Affective carrying combats loneliness, especially for people living alone or working from home. A carried cat is a gentle, warm presence.

## How to Carry Your Cat Safely

### Respect the Cat's Consent

A cat should never be carried by force. Let them come to you:

1. Offer contact (outstretched hand)
2. Let the cat rub against you and climb
3. Guide their movement
4. Watch for signs of acceptance (purring, relaxation)

### Signs That the Cat Doesn't Want to Be Carried

- Wagging tail
- Ears flattened back
- Attempt to jump
- Rigid body

If your cat shows these signs, don't force it. Try again later.

## Affective Carrying at Different Life Stages

### Kitten (2-12 months)

This is the best time to start carrying. The kitten gets used to contact and forms a lasting bond.

### Adult Cat (1-7 years)

Carrying strengthens an already established bond. Ideal for active cats who like to observe the world.

### Senior Cat (7+ years)

Carrying helps older cats that move less. The warmth soothes the joints and contact reduces aging-related anxiety.

## FAQ

### My cat doesn't like being carried, can I change that?

Yes, with a gradual approach. Start with 30 seconds of contact, slowly increasing. Associate carrying with a treat. Never force it.

### How long per day should I carry my cat?

15 to 30 minutes a day is sufficient. Some cats like to be carried for 2-3 hours, others prefer short, frequent sessions.

### Is carrying suitable for all cats?

Yes, but each cat has its own style. Some like to be carried in a "kangaroo" position (in a pouch), others on the shoulder, others against the chest. Observe your cat's preferences.
