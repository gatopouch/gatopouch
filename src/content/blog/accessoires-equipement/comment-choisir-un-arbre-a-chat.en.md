# How to Choose the Right Cat Tree for Your Feline

A cat tree is the number one investment for your indoor feline's well-being. It combines a scratching post, an observation perch, a hiding place, and a play area. But with the multitude of models available, how do you choose? Here is our complete guide to making the right choice.

## Why a Cat Tree is Essential

### The Natural Need to Climb

In the wild, cats climb to:

- **Observe** their territory from a height (surveillance position)
- **Hunt** prey from an elevated post
- **Take refuge** from predators
- **Mark** their territory (scratching)

Deprived of height, a cat develops stress, boredom, and behavioral problems.

### An Alternative to the Sofa and Furniture

A cat tree with an integrated scratching post prevents your cat from scratching your furniture. Cats prefer rough materials (sisal, cardboard) over fabric.

## Criteria for Choosing a Cat Tree

### 1. The Height

Height is the number one criterion. The taller the tree, the happier your cat will be.

| Cat | Recommended Height |
|------|---------------------|
| Kitten | 60-90 cm |
| Average Cat | 100-150 cm |
| Large Cat (Maine Coon) | 150-200 cm |
| Multi-cat Household | 180-250 cm |

### 2. Stability

A wobbly tree will scare the cat. Check for:

- A wide and heavy base
- Thick posts (minimum 10 cm diameter)
- Quality materials (solid wood preferred)
- The option to secure it to the wall for large models

### 3. The Materials

#### The Scratching Post (Sisal)

- Prefer natural sisal (rough)
- Avoid synthetics (too smooth)
- Check the height of the scratching post (minimum 50 cm for an adult cat)

#### The Platforms

- Wood with thick carpet (easy to replace)
- Avoid velvet (the cat can't scratch it)
- Removable and washable fabric (a plus)

#### The Structure

- Solid wood (the most durable)
- Cardboard (economical but less durable)
- Metal (ultra-stable but less natural)

### 4. The Number of Platforms

For one cat, 2-3 platforms are sufficient. For multiple cats, plan for:

- 1 platform per cat + 1 hiding spot (house or hammock)
- Staggered levels to make climbing easier
- Varied heights for observing at different levels

### 5. The Accessories

Options to consider:

- **Enclosed House**: a refuge for shy cats
- **Hanging Hammock**: appreciated by light cats
- **Hanging Toys**: playful stimulation
- **Integrated Feeder**: for feeding at a height
- **Wall Shelf**: to extend the territory

## Mistakes to Avoid

### Buying One That's Too Small

A 60 cm tree for an adult cat is useless. A cat wants to climb high.

### Choosing an Unstable Model

Cheap trees are often light and unstable. The cat won't use them.

### Ignoring the Scratching Posts

A tree without a scratching post is only for climbing. The cat needs both functions.

### Not Respecting the Cat's Personality

A shy cat prefers an enclosed house. An extroverted cat loves open platforms.

## How Much to Invest?

| Budget | Type of Tree |
|--------|--------------|
| 30-60€ | Small basic model (kitten or small cat) |
| 80-150€ | Good mid-range (adult cat) |
| 200-400€ | High-end (large cat, multi-cat household) |
| 500€+ | Custom-made or premium tree |

**Our advice**: Invest in quality from the start. A good tree lasts 10 years; a cheap model needs replacing every 2 years.

## Alternatives to the Cat Tree

If space is limited, consider:

### Wall Shelves

- Installed in a staircase pattern on the wall
- Stable and modular
- Cheaper than a large tree

### Vertical Scratching Posts

- Simple sisal post
- Ideal for a studio apartment
- 50-100€ for a good model

### Multi-functional Furniture

- Sofa with an integrated scratching post
- Tables with a cat platform
- Elevated, accessible furniture

## FAQ

### At what age should I get a cat tree?

As early as 2-3 months. The kitten learns to climb and scratch in the right place. Start with a basic model, then get a larger one when it reaches adulthood.

### My cat isn't using its tree, what should I do?

- Place it near a window
- Put treats on the platforms
- Play with it on the tree (fishing rod)
- Check the stability (an unstable tree = not used)

### Should I clean the cat tree?

Yes, once a month. Vacuum the fur, check the screws, and replace worn-out sisal rope. Removable covers = easy cleaning.

## Conclusion

A cat tree is the most profitable investment for your feline's happiness. It combines a natural need (to climb), a behavior (to scratch), and comfort (a refuge). Take the time to choose a model that is adapted to your cat's size and personality.
