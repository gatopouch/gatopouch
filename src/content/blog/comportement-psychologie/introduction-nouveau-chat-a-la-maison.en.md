# Introducing a New Cat to the Home: Guide

Introducing a new cat is a delicate moment that requires patience and a methodical approach. With the right steps, you can turn this transition into a harmonious cohabitation between your felines.

## Isolate the New Cat: The First Crucial Step

For the first 3 to 5 days, isolate the new cat in a separate room with its litter box, food, water, and a comfortable shelter. This step allows your new arrival to get used to the sounds and smells of the house without stress. Cats are territorial, and this separation prevents conflicts by giving each animal its own secure space. Change rooms from time to time so the new cat can explore other areas under supervision.

## Swap Scents to Familiarize the Cats

Cats communicate a lot through scents. Exchange the blankets, toys, or cloths used by each cat to mix their scents. You can also use a glove to rub one cat's face and leave the scent on the other's furniture. This technique helps reduce the perception of the "intruder" and create familiarity without direct contact. Repeat this exercise several times a day to speed up the acclimatization.

## Gradual, Supervised Meetings

After a week of isolation, start with short meetings (5-10 minutes) in a neutral space, like a bathroom. Monitor their behavior: growling, hissing, or attacks require an immediate interruption. Gradually increase the duration of the sessions and offer distractions like treats or interactive toys. Let them interact freely only when signs of relaxation appear (tails up, calm meows).

### FAQ

**Q: How long does it take for cats to get used to each other?**  
A: The duration varies from a few days to several weeks. Some cats adapt quickly, while others need more time. Be patient and respect the pace of each animal.

**Q: Can cats cohabitate without ever liking each other?**  
A: Yes, peaceful cohabitation is possible even without mutual affection. The important thing is that they tolerate each other's presence without aggression, sharing the space respectfully.
