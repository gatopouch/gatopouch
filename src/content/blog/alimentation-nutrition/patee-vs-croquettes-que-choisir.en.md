The question of cat food is at the heart of every owner's concerns: wet food or dry food, which option is best suited for your four-legged companion's diet? Between the two formats, the differences are notable in terms of composition, benefits, and your feline's specific needs. In this article, we help you see things more clearly to make the best choice for your cat.

## The fundamental differences between wet food and dry food

Wet food and dry food represent two very distinct forms of feeding, each with its own characteristics. Understanding these differences is essential for meeting your cat's nutritional needs.

### Composition and moisture

Wet food is a moist food, with a water content generally between 70% and 80%. This makes it a valuable source of hydration, particularly important for cats that drink little. Dry food, on the other hand, is dry and contains only about 10% water. Its composition is more concentrated in protein and calories, but it requires additional hydration from your cat.

### Texture and palatability

The texture of wet food is often more appealing to cats, as it resembles the fresh prey they hunt in the wild. Dry food, being harder, can help clean teeth and limit tartar, but some cats may reject it if it's too dry or bland.

## The advantages of wet food for cats

Wet food offers several assets that make it an interesting choice for many cat owners.

### Optimal hydration

With its high water content, wet food helps prevent kidney and urinary problems, which are common in cats. This is particularly beneficial for older cats or those that tend not to drink enough.

### Palatability and digestibility

Wet food is often easier to digest due to its fine, moist texture. It is also ideal for picky cats or those with dental problems, as it does not require intense chewing.

### Variety of flavors

Available in multiple flavors (chicken, beef, fish, etc.), wet food allows you to vary your cat's menu and stimulate its appetite.

## The advantages of dry food for cats

Dry food also offers significant benefits, particularly in terms of convenience and dental health.

### Convenience and storage

Dry food lasts longer once opened and is easier to portion. It is also ideal for owners who leave their cat alone during the day.

### Dental health

The crunching of dry food helps reduce tartar and massage the gums, thus contributing to better dental hygiene. Some dry foods are even enriched with anti-tartar agents.

### Weight control

Dry food allows for better portion control, which is useful for overweight cats or those prone to obesity.

## How to choose between wet food and dry food?

The choice between wet food and dry food depends on several factors related to your cat and your lifestyle.

### Based on your cat's needs

- **Young or active cat**: Energetic dry food may be suitable.
- **Older cat or with kidney problems**: Wet food is often preferred for its water content.
- **Picky cat or with sensitive teeth**: Wet food is easier to consume.

### Based on your budget and time

- **Practical and economical**: Dry food is often more affordable and easier to manage.
- **Quality and variety**: Wet food offers a diet closer to a natural diet, but it can be more expensive.

### Mixed solution: wet food and dry food

Many owners choose to combine both: wet food in the evening for hydration and dry food during the day for convenience. This combination allows you to enjoy the benefits of both formats.

## FAQ

### Is wet food better than dry food for my cat's health?

Wet food is particularly beneficial for hydration and digestibility, while dry food is practical for dental health. The choice depends on your cat's specific needs.

### Can I feed my cat only wet food?

Yes, but you must ensure that the wet food is balanced and covers all of your cat's nutritional needs. A mixed diet is often recommended to balance the benefits of both formats.

### Is dry food cheaper than wet food?

Generally, yes. Dry food is often more affordable per kilogram than wet food, but this depends on the quality and brand chosen.
