# Dietary Transition: Changing Kibble Safely

Changing your cat's food is sometimes necessary, but an ill-prepared transition can cause digestive issues. Here’s how to proceed gently to ensure your feline's well-being.

## Why a gradual transition?

Your cat's digestive system is delicate and accustomed to a specific composition. A sudden change in kibble can cause diarrhea, vomiting, or a loss of appetite. A gradual transition allows its microbiome to adapt progressively to the new nutrients, thus avoiding digestive shocks. Even if your cat seems healthy, its body needs time to adjust to the proteins, fibers, and fats of the new formula.

## The 7-step simple method

The golden rule is to take 7 to 10 days, progressively mixing the old and new kibbles. Start with 75% old and 25% new, then increase the proportion of new kibble every 2-3 days. For example:
- Days 1-3: 75% old / 25% new
- Days 4-6: 50% / 50%
- Days 7-10: 25% old / 75% new
Finish with 100% new kibble once your cat is fully accustomed. Monitor its stools and appetite during this period.

## Signs to watch for during the transition

Some cats adapt quickly, while others need more time. Be vigilant for persistent diarrhea, repeated vomiting, or a total refusal of food. If these symptoms appear, slow down the transition pace or consult your veterinarian. A cat stressed by the change may also drink less or become lethargic. Hydration remains crucial: don't hesitate to offer fresh water or wet food as a supplement.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I change kibbles faster if my cat seems to like them?**
**A:** Even if your cat seems to enjoy the new kibble, its digestive system needs time. Accelerate the transition only if your veterinarian recommends it, for example for medical reasons.

**Q: Can treats help with the transition?**
**A:** Yes, appealing treats can encourage your cat to taste the new kibble. Choose treats that are compatible with its new diet to avoid digestive conflicts.
