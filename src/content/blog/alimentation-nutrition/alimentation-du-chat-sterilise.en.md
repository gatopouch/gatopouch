# Feeding a Sterilized Cat: Avoiding Weight Gain

Spaying or neutering alters your cat's metabolism, making it more prone to weight gain. With a few dietary adjustments, you can maintain its ideal weight and health.

## Why do sterilized cats gain weight?

After the procedure, your cat's metabolism slows down by 20 to 30%, while its appetite can increase. This combination promotes weight gain, especially if the diet remains unchanged. The hormones that regulate hunger disappear, pushing your cat to eat more than it needs. Excess weight can lead to joint problems, diabetes, or heart disease. Understanding this change is the first step in adapting its diet.

## Choosing the right food

Opt for a special "sterilized cat" kibble or wet food, formulated with fewer calories but rich in protein to preserve muscle mass. Foods high in fiber (such as beet pulp or wheat bran) help keep your cat feeling full longer. Avoid high-calorie treats: prefer lean meat pieces or cooked vegetables like carrots. A balanced diet is essential to prevent weight gain while providing the necessary energy.

## Controlling portions and feeding schedules

Divide the daily ration into 2 to 3 small meals instead of one large one to avoid overfeeding. Use a food scale to measure portions precisely, as even 10% too much can cause your cat to gain weight over time. If your cat constantly begs, offer it food-dispensing toys or puzzles to stimulate it mentally without adding calories. Regularly monitoring its weight (once a month) will allow you to adjust its diet if necessary.

## FAQ

**Q: My sterilized cat has already gained weight, what should I do?**  
A: Consult your veterinarian to create a gradual weight loss plan. Slightly reduce portions (10-15%) and increase its physical activity with interactive games.

**Q: Are "light" foods sufficient?**  
A: No, light foods can be low in protein and high in carbohydrates. Prioritize formulas balanced in protein, fiber, and essential nutrients to prevent muscle loss.
